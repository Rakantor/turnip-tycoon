# German (Deutsch)

- Use **du**, like the game, written in lower case (du, dein, dir). Imperatives use the du form: „Trag den Preis ein“.
- Game terms, from the game’s German text: **Rüben** (singular Rübe), **Sternis** (singular Sterni), **Jorna**, **Nooks Laden**, **Nepp und Schlepp**, **Insel**. Never write Daisy Mae, Nook’s Cranny, bells or Glocken.
- Pattern names, as German players know them from Turnip Prophet: **Schwankend**, **Stark ansteigend**, **Absteigend**, **Leicht ansteigend**. In a sentence, rephrase freely: „eine Woche mit starkem Anstieg“.
- Half-days are compound nouns: **Montagvormittag**, **Samstagnachmittag**; in a sentence „am Montagvormittag“. Short labels: „Mo. vorm.“, „Sa. nachm.“; on their own, „vorm.“ and „nachm.“.
- Times use the 24-hour clock: „8 Uhr“, „22 Uhr“, „12 Uhr“ (or „mittags“).
- Quotation marks are „…“. The app formats percentages with a space („42 %“); don’t add your own percent signs to numbers it inserts.
- With player names, avoid genitive endings: „die Insel von {name}“ reads well for every name, „{name}s Insel“ doesn’t.
- Keep sentences short and natural rather than literal. German runs longer than English, so be economical on buttons, chips and headings.
- Prefer plain German over anglicisms where one exists („Anstieg“ rather than „Spike“, „Prognose“ rather than „Forecast“), but keep established app words like „App“ and „Link“.
