# French (Français)

- Use **tu**, like the game. Speak to a group with vous: “Comparez vos prix…”.
- Game terms, from the game’s French text: **navets** (singular navet), **clochettes** (singular clochette), **Porcelette**, **la Boutique Nook**, **Méli et Mélo**, **île**. Never write Daisy Mae, Nook’s Cranny, bells or cloches.
- Unsold turnips **pourrissent** (the game’s item is “lot de navets pourris”): “Les navets pourrissent le dimanche”, “Navets pourris”.
- Pattern names, as French players know them from Turnip Prophet: **Variable**, **Grand pic**, **Décroissante**, **Petit pic**. A pattern is a **courbe**; in a sentence, “une semaine à grand pic”, “une semaine décroissante”.
- Half-days are “lundi matin” and “lundi après-midi”, lower case, without a preposition in a sentence: “Ton meilleur prix était lundi matin”. Short labels and columns use AM and PM, as Turnip Prophet does: “lun. AM”, “sam. PM”.
- Times: “8 h”, “22 h”, “midi”.
- Prices the app inserts are bare numbers that stand for clochettes, so they take feminine plural words: “les {price} de {name}”, “tes {price}”, “les {price} que tu as payées”.
- Typography: « … » with no-break spaces inside, a no-break space before “:”, and a narrow no-break space before “;”, “!” and “?”.
