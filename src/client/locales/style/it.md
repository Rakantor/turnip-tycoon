# Italian (Italiano)

- Use **tu**, like the game. Speak to a group with voi: “Confrontate i vostri prezzi…”. Prefer wording that doesn’t depend on the player’s gender: “Ti diamo il benvenuto”, “Ora fai parte di {groupName}”.
- Game terms, from the game’s Italian text: **rape** (singular rapa), **stelline** (singular stellina), **Brunella**, **la Bottega di Nook**, **Mirco e Marco** (Timmy is Mirco, Tommy is Marco), **isola**. Never write Daisy Mae, Nook’s Cranny, bells or campanelle.
- Unsold turnips **marciscono** (the game’s item is “mazzo di rape marce”): “Le rape marciscono la domenica”, “Rape marce”.
- Pattern names, as Italian players know them from Turnip Prophet: **Oscillante**, **Grande picco**, **Decrescente**, **Piccolo picco**. A pattern is an **andamento**; in a sentence, “una settimana con un grande picco”, “una settimana decrescente”.
- Half-days are “lunedì mattina” and “lunedì pomeriggio”, lower case, usually without a preposition in a sentence: “è stato di 300 stelline lunedì mattina”. Short labels and columns use AM and PM, as Turnip Prophet does: “lun. AM”, “sab. PM”.
- Times use the 24-hour clock: “8:00”, “22:00”, “mezzogiorno”.
- Prices the app inserts are bare numbers that stand for stelline, so they take feminine plural words: “le {price} di {name}”, “le tue {price}”.
- Use «…» for quotations and a colon where English uses a dash.
