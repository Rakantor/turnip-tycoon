# Spanish (Español)

- One Spanish for Spain and Latin America, like the game’s own text, which uses the same names in both. Use **tú**, never vos or vosotros: when the text speaks to a group, rephrase (“Compara tus precios con los de tus amigos…”). Avoid words that differ by region: “teléfono” rather than móvil or celular, “PC” rather than ordenador or computadora.
- Game terms, from the game’s Spanish text: **nabos** (singular nabo), **bayas** (singular baya), **Juliana**, **MiniNook** (no article: “antes de que cierre MiniNook”), **Tendo y Nendo**, **isla**. Never write Daisy Mae, Nook’s Cranny, bells or campanas.
- Unsold turnips **se pasan** (the game’s item is “manojo pasado”): “Los nabos se pasan el domingo”, “Nabos pasados”.
- Pattern names, as Spanish players know them from Turnip Prophet: **Fluctuante**, **Pico alto**, **Decreciente**, **Pico moderado**. A pattern is a **patrón**; in a sentence, “una semana de pico alto”, “una semana decreciente”.
- Half-days are “lunes por la mañana” and “lunes por la tarde”, lower case like all weekdays, with the article in a sentence: “el lunes por la mañana”. Short labels and columns use AM and PM, as Turnip Prophet does: “lun. AM”, “sáb. PM”.
- Times use the 24-hour clock: “8:00”, “22:00”, “mediodía”.
- Prices the app inserts are bare numbers that stand for bayas, so they take feminine plural words: “las {price} de {name}”, “tus {price}”.
- Use «…» for quotations, ¿…? and ¡…!, and a colon where English uses a dash.
