# Translating Turnip Tycoon

Turnip Tycoon helps Animal Crossing: New Horizons players track turnip prices, see where the week is heading, and compare prices with friends. Most people use it on a phone, in short visits while they play.

## Voice

- Friendly, plain, and short. Write the way a helpful fellow player talks, not like a bank or a manual.
- The mascot speaks in the first person in the forecast bubble (“I’ll forecast your week”). Keep that warmth, and the occasional light exclamation (“Hang on!”), without getting childish.
- Address the player the way the game does in your language.
- Say the same thing as the English: don’t add advice, drop a caveat, or soften a warning. Wording about data, deletion, and privacy must stay exact.

## Game terms

Use the game’s own names in your language for everything from Animal Crossing: characters, shops, the currency, and items. The glossary lists them, with sources; never keep an English game name unless the official name in your language is English. “Turnip Tycoon” is the app’s name and is never translated.

## Placeholders, tags and plurals

- `{name}`, `{price}`, `{0}` and the like are filled in by the app. Copy them exactly, never translate inside the braces, and move them wherever your sentence needs them. A comment such as `placeholder {0}: group.name` tells you what a numbered one holds.
- `<0>…</0>`, `<1>…</1>` wrap text that is emphasized or a link. Keep every pair, translate the text inside, and move them freely. A self-closing `<0/>` must stay too.
- `{count, plural, one {…} other {…}}` chooses a sentence by number. Keep the structure, translate each branch, and use the plural categories your language needs (add `few` or `many` where it has them). `#` is where the number goes.
- Numbers, percentages, dates and weekday names are formatted by the app for your language; don’t rewrite them.
- Names of players, groups and devices come from players and arrive through placeholders. Prefer sentence shapes that work with any name, rather than possessive endings that depend on how the name is spelled.

## Fit and typography

- Space is tight on phones. Buttons, tags, chips, column headings and short labels should stay about as short as the English. Comments say where text appears.
- Use your language’s quotation marks, a typographic apostrophe (’), an ellipsis character (…), and an en dash (–) for ranges.
- Nook’s Cranny opens 8 AM–10 PM, and prices change at noon. Write times the way your language usually does.
